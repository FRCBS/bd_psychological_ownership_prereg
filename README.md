# "Psychological ownership and identity motives in blood donation" - repetition in Finland
Preregistration code for repetition of "Psychological ownership and identity motives in blood donation" (https://doi.org/10.1111/vox.13479).


What did we study?

We wanted to understand whether feeling a sense of ownership (called psychological ownership) over blood donation could impact blood donors’ motivation to continue donating. Previous research in Australia suggested that donors who felt a stronger sense of psychological ownership were more likely to see blood donation as an important part of who they are (self-identity) and thus intend to donate again. We examined whether the same pattern was present among Finnish blood donors.


What did we find?

Responses from 492 active Finnish blood donors showed that having donated more frequently in the past was positively associated with greater psychological ownership, which, in turn, was associated with increased self-identity as a blood donor and greater intention to donate blood in the future. These findings were largely consistent with previous Australian research.


Why is this important?

Understanding the psychological factors that encourage people to keep donating blood can help blood services develop strategies to support long-term donor retention. Our findings suggest that fostering a sense of ownership toward blood donation may play a role in encouraging ongoing donation.
